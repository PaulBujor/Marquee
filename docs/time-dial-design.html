Rewind: five ways to turn a dial through the event log

A design document, not an implementation. The rewind dial currently maps the entire log onto one half-circle, then trades precision for range with a distance-based decimator. This document measures that trade, names the hardware it comes from, and lays out five distinct interaction models concrete enough to build — with the one change that makes all of them better, and the questions that are the user's to answer.

1The problem, measured

The ask: "I expect to be able to rotate the dial multiple times over so I can have fine control over the events, instead of just half the circle we have now." That is three complaints in one sentence, and they resolve at different layers.

  • Range is tied to angle. SWEEP = 180 maps the whole ordered log onto half a face. At RADIUS = 84 the usable arc is π·84 ≈ 264 px of thumb travel — for the whole log, forever.
  • Precision is paid for with decimation. DETENT_CAPACITY = 120 means one detent every 264 / 120 ≈ 2.2 px of arc. With ≈800 events in the log, one detent is 800 / 120 ≈ 6.7 events (densityFor(800) = 7; the unit test cites the same curve at densityFor(20_000) = 167). One thumb-pixel ≈ 3 events.
  • The decimation is by distance, not by speed. densityFor(distance) makes coarseness a function of where you are in the log. It is applied while you are standing still, reading, trying to land exactly. That is the smell: the control is coarse precisely where the user wants it fine.

Two structural problems under the geometry

  • The position is a fraction, so it cannot survive a reload. The page derives progress from back, and the dial hands back a 0→1 fraction that the page re-derives back from (backFromProgress(log.length, position)). Any change to the log — another device's event arriving through sync.revision — silently moves the dial, because the same fraction of a larger log is a different event. An identity-anchored dial cannot be expressed in this contract at all.
  • The accessible value is a percentage of the log. aria-valuenow is round(progress × 100) and aria-valuemax is 1. A screen reader says "12, slider" where the useful thing to say is "3 events back, S1E3 watched, Breaking Bad". The fraction leaked into the accessibility tree because it is the only thing the component knows.
The one-line thesis. Precision should be a function of how carefully you turn, not of how far back you are. Today it is the second; every option below makes it the first — and the cheapest part of every option is deleting densityFor and DETENT_CAPACITY.

2Precedents worth citing

Nothing here is invented. Every part of this problem — unbounded rotation, discrete targets, range versus precision on one control — was solved physically, decades before it was solved on glass.

Mechanism What it is What transfers What does not
Broadcast jog / shuttle wheel A sprung wheel with two regimes: jog steps exactly one frame per detent, shuttle winds continuously as you push against spring tension. One control, two ratios, no mode switch. Unbounded rotation. Operators trust it for frame-accurate trims and for crossing an hour of tape — the closest analogue to this problem that exists. Shuttle's rate comes from displacement of a lever with real resistance. A touchscreen gives no displacement, only velocity and acceleration — a worse, noisier signal.
Endless rotary encoder No end stops; reports only quadrature deltas. The host device owns the value. (Volume knobs, CNC manual pulse generators.) The exact contract this component is missing: step(delta), not position(fraction). The knob never knows what it controls. An encoder has no absolute position, so the panel must print the value — which is why the hub readout is load-bearing, not decoration.
Combination lock / safe dial ~100 divisions a turn, deliberate multiple revolutions, detents you feel through a glove. Multiple turns are learnable when each turn has a name — "three left, two right". It also names the failure: counting laps is the user's memory burden, so a multi-turn dial must show the lap. The precision of 100 divisions a turn is not needed. We are not dialling a secret; we are landing on one event among hundreds.
Mechanical counter / odometer The digits move; nothing rotates. Carries are automatic and free. The value carries the range, not the angle. Six digits cover 10⁶ with perfect resolution at the last one, and no visual ever repeats. Reading six digits sequentially is slow. Good for confirmation, poor for scrubbing; hence a hub readout, not a face of digits.
Camera command dial A clicky dial with a fixed ratio per click, plus a mode dial that changes what the clicks mean. Gearing as an explicit control is legitimate and learnable — photographers choose it, and it never surprises them. A hidden gear is a support burden. If the ratio can change under the user, it has to be visible at all times.
Scroll wheel with momentum Fling, coast with exponential decay, settle on a snap point. Momentum is how a small control crosses a big list cheaply, and the fling→coast→snap math is the same integral used below. Pixels are continuous and free. Our targets are discrete and each one is a candidate write, so overshoot must return, not just stop.

One more, from outside the list: film scrubbing in a video player. It is the same user need, and it converged on a thumbnail strip plus positional precision on release (release-to-scrub, then frame step). That is a jump affordance bolted onto a coarse drag — and it is a hint at the recommendation below.

3The invariant: what one turn means

Before the models, four decisions that should hold in all of them. They are what makes the options comparable rather than five unrelated sketches.

3.1 A detent is 15° — 24 to a turn (proposal)

At RADIUS = 84 a 15° detent is 84 × 0.2618 ≈ 22 px of arc: coarse enough to feel with a thumb, fine enough to land on. 24 detents a turn is the number every option below assumes. It is a proposal, not a measurement — the only measured thing here is that today's 2.2 px pitch is too small to resolve.

3.2 At rest, one detent is exactly one event — everywhere in the log

densityFor and DETENT_CAPACITY go. Coarseness is allowed only while winding, and then it is a function of speed, not position. This single sentence is the difference between "a bigger dial" (a dead end) and a control that still works at 10,000 events.

3.3 Zoom is not a scale — it is a ladder of units

An unbounded list cannot be zoomed continuously: a screen is not unbounded, so a continuous scale has to re-map "fraction of the whole log" onto "fraction of the screen", which is exactly the operation that gets coarser as the log grows. Instead, zoom selects a unit the detent walks, from a short ladder — event, ten events, hour, day, week, month — each of which is traversable in a bounded number of detents per screen. The existing REWIND_STOPS ladder (Now · 15m · 1h · 6h · 1d · 3d · 1w · 1mo) is already this idea, used only in time mode; the proposal is to make it the single ladder both modes read.

3.4 Zoom preserves identity, never a fraction

The anchor is the value itself: the event index in event mode, the cutoff in ms in time mode. Both are absolute, so changing the unit changes nothing about where you are — the event you were on stays the event you are on, with more or fewer neighbours per turn. This is the crux, and it is cheap: it is the difference between storing back: number and storing progress: number and deriving back from it.

The bounded-screen consequence. With 3.4 in place, the dial never draws "position within the whole log" as an angle. What it draws is the window — the current unit and its immediate neighbours — plus, at most, a track showing coarse extent with a marker (a minimap), never a handle. Any option that tries to draw the whole log as an angle has rebuilt today's problem at a larger radius.

4Five models

Each is a different answer to one question — where does the range come from, if a turn is fixed precision? — not a variation on a single wheel. They are ordered from "the smallest change to what exists" to "rotation is the wrong control".

A

Endless jog with a gear lever

The dial becomes an endless encoder — 24 detents a turn, one event per detent — and range comes from a gear the user sets (1× / 8× / 64×) instead of from the angle.

Mapping
Unbounded integer detents; value = clamp(anchor + detents × gear). At 800 events: 1× → 33.3 turns, 8× → 4.2 turns, 64× → 0.52 turns. The gear multiplies the step only, so changing it never moves you.
Visual state
The transform is (detents % 24) × 15° — one lap, forever, no re-basing and no float drift (see §8). The lap count lives in a chip ("turn 07"), the gear in a pill ("8×"), and the choice of chip over angle is the whole trick: the wheel stays a physical object while the number carries the depth.
Feel
Clicky and predictable — "I know how far one turn is". No momentum: a gear is a decision instead of a guess, and that predictability is exactly what a settled wheel is for.
Zoom
The gear is the zoom, and it is resolution-only. Because the value is an integer event index, switching 1× ↔ 64× leaves you exactly where you were. This is the cheapest correct zoom in the document.
Haptics
One 10 ms pulse per detent; a distinct [8, 30, 8] when the gear changes.
Trade-off
Two controls where the user asked for one. A gear left on 64× overshoots by 64 events per detent, and it is invisible until it bites.
Build cost
Smallest of the five. Add unbounded detent accumulation, delete densityFor/DETENT_CAPACITY, keep ondetent, add a three-way pill. The existing Fine/Shift modifier becomes gear 1×, so that rename is nearly free.
Hard part
It does not fix the complaint's feel — it fixes precision by adding a mode, and a mode is a tax on every use. Fine for a power user; a permanent small papercut.
B

Jog / shuttle

One wheel, two regimes: below about a turn a second every detent is one event; above it the wheel stops stepping and winds, at a rate driven by how fast it is spinning.

Mapping
24 detents a turn. Jog: 1 event per detent (24 events a second at one turn a second). Wind: rate = min(400, 24 · |ω|^1.6) events a second, for ω in turns a second — 24 at one turn a second (continuous with jog), ≈220 at four, capped at 400, which crosses 800 events in about 2 seconds. A proposal; the exponent is the tuning knob.
Momentum
A flick is just wind that outlives the finger: v *= exp(−dt/τ) with τ ≈ 280 ms, plus a detent friction floor below ~0.5 detents a second so it always stops on a detent.
Not sliding past
Two rules. (1) Wind cannot settle between detents: on release the value snaps to the nearest one with a 120 ms ease, and if the overshoot was 8 events or more the panel offers a "−9" back-step button rather than making the user hunt. (2) Tap to brake: any pointerdown during coast kills the velocity instantly, no deceleration curve.
Visual
During wind, per-event rendering is suspended: the rim becomes a dashed arc whose dash pitch encodes speed, the hub reads "×12", and the panel replays once, on settle. This is where decimation belongs — inside the wind, by speed, instead of permanently, by distance.
Zoom
None. Range is speed, so the unit ladder (§3.3) is the only zoom, and anchoring is free because the value is an event index.
Haptics
Jog: 10 ms per detent. Wind: the per-detent pulse stops being possible above ~25 a second (the existing MIN_GAP_MS = 40 ceiling) — so the switch into wind gets its own pattern, and the stop gets one more.
Trade-off
Mistuning. A super-linear rate is deliberate at speed and twitchy in the middle of the range, and it is tuned by feel once, then frozen. Reduced motion loses the primary wind feedback (no blur, no fast rim), so that path needs a different display, not a disabled one.
Build cost
Medium. A rAF loop, velocity from the last two pointer samples, quantised rendering, and a page that must survive ~60 value commits a second while winding.
Hard part
Velocity→rate has no physical ground truth on a touchscreen. There is no lever to push, so the mapping is an invention that will feel arbitrary to someone, and it cannot be discovered by the user — only tuned by us.
C

Wrap by unit: each lap has a name

The wheel never turns past one lap — it wraps at a named boundary (a day), and the hub counts the wraps, so thirty turns is not a blind disc but "three days back, second lap".

Mapping
24 detents a turn; the angle is the position within the unit (a day, or 24 events in event mode); crossing the unit's end rolls the unit index by one at 12 o'clock, with a wrap tick. Unbounded value, bounded angle: there is no re-basing maths at all — the transform is always (positionInUnit / 24) × 360°.
Momentum
Gentle, and deliberately interrupted: the wrap boundary acts as a detent plateau, so a fast wind slows at every day. In a journal-shaped log that is a feature — days are how people remember — but it does cap the wind at one unit per lap.
Visual
Two concentric bands: the inner one carries the 24 detents and the hand; the outer carries the last few unit names ("Tue · Wed 3 Jun · Thu") with the current unit lit. The wrap is a short roll animation at the top, matched to the announcement so the eye and the haptic agree.
Zoom
The ladder chooses the unit, and the anchor is the timestamp you are on, so "day" → "hour" keeps you at the same moment on a finer wheel. This is the option where the §3.4 invariant feels most natural, because the unit is already named on the rim.
Jumping
The hub doubles as the jump: tap it to open a small drum of units ("Wed 3 Jun", "Tue 2 Jun"…), which also answers "how did I get here" and "how do I get back".
Haptics
10 ms per detent; [10, 20, 10] on a wrap; [12, 40, 12] on reaching the present.
Trade-off
Unequal laps. A quiet day holds 3 events and a binge evening holds 200, so one lap buys a very different distance depending on where you are — and users read absolute angle as distance. The angle is honest about time and dishonest about events.
Build cost
Medium. Wrap detection, a unit index, and a wrap animation. No new momentum maths.
Hard part
The wheel becomes calendar-locked in event mode: at 24 events a wrap, an 800-event log wraps 33 times with no meaning attached, so the unit must be the day — and then the mapping is "day per lap", which is not the event-precision the user asked for. C is strongest as a layer on another option, not as one.
D

The log turns under a fixed pointer

Invert the mechanism: the rim carries the events themselves and turns under a fixed pointer at 12 o'clock, so a repeating angle is fine because the content under the pointer never repeats.

Mapping
24 event slivers a lap; one detent, one event; orientation is −detents × 15° applied to the ring group, not to a hand. Unbounded and unrebased, because the thing rotating is content, not an indicator.
Why it survives 30 turns
The "blind spinning disc" objection attacks the indicator: its angle repeats, so after a lap you learn nothing from it. A ring of posters repeats its angle too — but at turn 30 the pointer is over different slivers, and the hub prints the depth ("03·07" = third turn, seventh event). Repetition of angle, novelty of content.
Momentum
The content smears: slivers streak tangentially while winding and snap into the pointer's gate on settle — the slot-machine reel, which is exactly a discrete target with momentum. Deceleration is capped so a sliver can never flash past at a rate that reads as a strobe.
Visual
Rim: 24 slivers, each a tick plus a poster colour plus an event glyph — texture, not text. Gate at 12: one full-width window holding the landed event's words. Hub: the lap odometer.
Zoom
The number of slivers a lap changes: 24 events, 12 hours, 31 days. This is the only option where zoom's anchor is geometrically trivial — the pointer never moves, so the thing under it stays under it by construction.
Trade-off
Cost, and the loss of a familiar affordance: people recognise a clock's hand. A rotating face reads as a reel — which is the right metaphor here, but it is a new one in this app.
Build cost
Highest. Per-sliver layout and keys, the smear effect, a gate that must stay legible while everything else moves, and a reduced-motion substitute for the smear.
Hard part
Legibility of 24 slivers on an 84 radius ring: each is ≈22 px wide, enough for a colour and a glyph, not a title. So the words must live in the gate — and a gate that changes content 24 times a lap is a lot of small type changes to keep from flickering.
E

Jump-first: rotation demoted

Rotation is the wrong control for a log this long — keep it as a fine nudge (±1 event, ±1 turn for a day) and put all range into jumps: a title palette, day chips, and "rewind to before this event".

Mapping
The wheel shows the local neighbourhood — the selected event and ±12 around it — rather than the log. One detent, one event, and a turn past the window's edge loads the next 24. Value is the event index; jumps set it absolutely.
Zoom
There is none: the wheel is permanently at "one event per detent", and the ladder of jump targets (event → day → title) is what changes scale. Anchoring is exact by construction, which is the point: no fraction, no recomputation.
Jumping
Three specifically: ⌘K palette (type a title → its events → "before this" — an 800-event scan is trivial client-side, and this is the only affordance that survives 10,000 events); day chips from the strip; and "rewind to before this" on the event panel, which already knows how — the per-event Undo in restore.ts is the same diff, one step earlier.
Momentum
Not needed. A window of 24 events is one turn.
Haptics
10 ms per detent, [30] as a wall at the window's edge — this is the option where "hitting a limit" is a real, frequent signal.
Trade-off
It is not what was asked for. The dial becomes a nudge wheel, and a nudge wheel that cannot travel teaches the user that the wheel is decoration.
Build cost
Low for the wheel (it only needs a window and a clamp), medium for the index (title → event positions, day buckets) and low for the panel.
Hard part
Two metaphors on one screen: a wheel that cannot travel, next to a search that can. The honest version of this option deletes the wheel entirely — which is a product decision, not a design one.

6Haptics: additive, never load-bearing

The only channel that exists today is haptics.tick() — 10 ms, floored to one pulse per 40 ms, so at most ~25 a second. That ceiling is the same number that makes a fast wind's per-detent pulse impossible, which is why the patterns below are about transitions, not counts.

Reality check before designing on it: navigator.vibrate does not exist on iOS at all, is largely ignored on desktop, and on Android needs the document to have been touched at least once (sticky activation). So every pattern below is a bonus.

Event Pattern (ms) Why that shape
Detent, at rest 10 The existing tick. A single short pulse reads as a click, not a buzz.
Entering wind [8, 30, 8] Two pulses: "detail is suspended, you are now travelling".
Gear / unit change [8, 30, 8], then 10 ms per detent as usual Same shape reused, so it means "the scale changed" everywhere.
Wrap into a new day (C) [10, 20, 10] Tighter than the gear pattern; it fires many times a session, so it must stay subtle.
Reaching the present [12, 40, 12] Deliberately the longest: this is the case where overshoot costs the most.
Limit (either end of the log) 40 A wall, not a click. With the detent tick suppressed past it, absence becomes the signal.

What the user loses without it

Not much, if the visual carries the same information — which is the requirement, not an aspiration. The one thing genuinely lost is the confirmation of a single detent while the eye is on the panel: watching the event text change is slower than feeling a click, so on iOS the digit in the hub must flash for ~90 ms on every detent, and the ring must step visibly. A phone with vibration off is the design target, not the degraded case.

A judgement call worth naming. A tick sound would be the better channel for a jog wheel (broadcast decks click because you can hear a detent), but an app that clicks on every scroll is a nuisance, and Web Audio needs a gesture to unlock. Left out deliberately; mentioned so it can be re-proposed with intent.

The screen-reader story

Change the role. A bounded slider with aria-valuemin="0" / aria-valuemax="1" is now a lie: the value has no maximum. The honest role is spinbutton — a value with arrow-key steps and no upper bound, which is exactly what this is. Then:

  • aria-valuetext = the sentence, in this order: "3 events back, S1E3 watched, Breaking Bad, yesterday". Distance first (that is what the control does), then the action, then which title, then when.
  • Drop the percentage entirely. "12%" of an event log is not a position anyone can use, and it is the current tree's only value.
  • Announcement policy: the focused spinbutton's own aria-valuetext is announced by the AT on change, so do not also mirror it into a live region — that double-speaks. The page's existing role="status" line stays for status ("Rewound 3 titles — 4 events written"), not for the dial value. During a wind, throttle the value announcements to the settled value only (one announcement on stop), or a screen-reader user gets 400 of them.
  • The film strip stays aria-hidden (it already is): it is motion, and the panel below is the accessible account.
  • Focus is one tab stop. The event panel's Undo is next; the jump palette button must be in the header, before the wheel, since a keyboard user who starts at the wheel should not have to spin.

prefers-reduced-motion

  • No settle transition (already the pattern: reduced gates the hand's 320 ms).
  • No smear, no dashed-speed rim, no strip translate: the wind renders at ≤5 Hz with the ring stepping instead of spinning, and the hub rate becomes text ("winding ×40").
  • Momentum is halved rather than removed — a flick still has to be able to reach something — and the brake gets more aggressive.
  • Announcements and the final value are identical: reduced motion changes the journey, never the destination.

8Implementation notes (Svelte 5)

8.1 What the current contract can and cannot say

// today — documented as "deliberately dumb about what a position means"
interface Props {
  progress: number;                                  // 0 = now, 1 = far end
  valueLabel: string;                                // spoken value
  labels?: readonly string[];
  onseek: (progress: number, fine: boolean) => void;  // pointer landed
  ondetent: (direction: 1 | -1, fine: boolean) => void; // one step
}

It cannot express a turn (only a direction), a rate, a jump, or an unbounded value — and the page's round trip back → progress → backFromProgress(...) is lossy every time the log changes length. Every option above stresses this the same way, so the contract change is not an option-specific cost: 

// proposal — the dial owns deltas and rate; the page owns truth
interface Props {
  /** Spoken and drawn value; the page composes it from the event under the hand. */
  valueText: string;
  /** Hub readout: the anchored moment, never a fraction of the log. */
  center: { label: string; sub: string };
  /** What one detent means, in words: "every event", "1 hour". */
  unitLabel: string;
  /** Integer detents since the gesture started: the only thing the wheel emits at rest. */
  onnudge: (detents: number, phase: 'rest' | 'wind') => void;
  /** Events per second while winding, signed; 0 on stop. Not called while jogging. */
  onwind?: (rate: number) => void;
  /** Settled: the page may commit once and render the diff. */
  onsettle: () => void;
  onjump?: (target: 'now' | 'oldest' | 'unit-up' | 'unit-down') => void;
}

onnudge collapses ondetent + onseek: a pointer drag is just detents at a high rate, so a flick and a drag stop being two code paths. This is also what makes option B cheap — it is A plus onwind.

8.2 Unbounded rotation without re-basing the transform

const DEG = 15;                       // 24 detents a turn
let detents = $state(0);              // unbounded, integer: the value
let phase = $state(0);                // raw angle remainder, kept inside ±DEG

const angle = $derived(((detents * DEG) % 360) * -1);   // the visual: bounded forever

function onPointerMove(e: PointerEvent) {
  phase += deltaDegrees(e);           // unwrapped across the ±180 seam
  const whole = Math.trunc(phase / DEG);
  if (whole === 0) return;
  phase -= whole * DEG;               // carry: phase never accumulates error
  detents += whole;
  onnudge(whole, 'rest');
}
  • No re-base, ever. The transform is a modulo of the value, so it is bounded by construction; nothing needs to "reset" the rotation when it passes 360°.
  • No float accumulation. The only float is phase, and it is subtracted back to within ±15° on every detent, so error cannot grow across 500 turns. Accumulating angles in one float and multiplying would drift visibly after a few thousand steps.
  • The seam. atan2 jumps at ±180°: diff the consecutive angles and wrap the difference into (−180, 180], not the absolute angle. Pointer angles are also noisy near the centre — ignore deltas measured inside ~20 px of the hub, and prefer the pointer's tangential component over the raw angle.
  • Pointer capture. setPointerCapture on pointerdown; release only if (el.hasPointerCapture(id)) — a pointercancel already releases it and releasing twice throws. The wrapper already carries touch-action: none; keep preventDefault on wheel.
  • Frame-rate independence. Integrate, never step per frame: carry += v * dt with dt from performance.now(), then emit Math.trunc(carry). A 30 Hz frame and a 120 Hz frame must travel the same distance for the same flick.
  • Commit budget. stateAtStep is O(n) and eventDiff is O(n) per touched title; at 800 events a full replay is tens of microseconds, so one commit per frame is affordable. The expensive part is the strip (touchedAfterStep allocating a Set over the tail) — skip it while winding. Measure at 5,000 events before shipping the wind.

8.3 The honest hard part, per option

Option What will actually take the time
A The gear as a mode: discoverability, and a visible ratio at all times.
B Tuning the velocity→rate curve and the settle, then never touching them again; plus the reduced-motion wind display.
C Deciding what a lap is when days are wildly unequal, and animating a wrap that does not look like a glitch.

9Recommendation

Take B's two regimes, with A's fixed detent as its rest state, and ship E's jumps as P0 — and delete densityFor/DETENT_CAPACITY in the first commit.

Concretely: 24 detents a turn at 15°; one detent is one event everywhere in the log at rest; above ~1 turn a second the wheel winds instead of stepping, capped at 400 events a second; the only zoom is a discrete unit ladder anchored on the event index; a palette plus day chips get you to a three-month-old event without spinning. Options C and D do not need to ship — C's lap labels are a cheap layer to add later if the wind proves disorienting, and D's inversion is a solution to a problem B removes.

Why this one:

  1. It is the user's sentence. "Rotate the dial multiple times over" — with one event a detent, a full turn is 24 events, so a small correction is a fraction of a turn and a deliberate hunt is a few turns. The half-circle limit was the bug.
  2. The thing to delete is the thing they complained about. Coarseness survives only as suspended rendering inside the wind, where it is invisible and free. That is the smallest honest fix, and it removes a block of tests rather than adding one.
  3. It resolves range-vs-precision by moving the trade onto speed. A is correct but pays with a mode the user must remember; B pays with a curve we must tune once. Tuning a curve beats asking the user to remember a gear.
  4. It upgrades the contract that everything else needs. Unbounded integer deltas + a settle callback is the same contract a linear tape, a minimap, or a rotation-free scrubber would need — so a bigger dial is not the only door, and the next redesign does not start over.
  5. With jumps, the wheel never has to cover the log. That is what lets the ratio stay human — 24 detents a turn — instead of degrading to 167 events a detent like densityFor(20_000) does today.

Shipping order, if it is agreed

  1. Contract + unbounded jog: 15° detents, one event each, modulo transform, spinbutton role, real value text. Delete the decimator. This alone answers the complaint.
  2. Wind: velocity, cap, settle, brake, reduced-motion path.
  3. Jumps: palette, day chips, "rewind to before this".
  4. Unit ladder unified across event and time modes (§3.3).
  5. Only then, if it still feels disorienting: C's lap labels, or a strip minimap for coarse extent.
What this recommendation deliberately does not do. It does not enlarge the dial. A larger radius buys a constant factor (2.2 px per detent today; ≈4.4 px at a doubled radius) against a log that grows without bound — at 10,000 events that is 0.1 px per event. Size is not the axis; the ratio is.

10Questions only you can answer

  1. Is a stop authoritative or exploratory? If landing on the wrong event and merely reading it is free, a fast wind can be forgiven its overshoot. If every stop is a candidate write (Undo sits right there, one tab away), the wind needs a confirm gate — which kills momentum. Which is it in practice?
  2. How long will the log actually get? It is ≈800 events today. Under ≈2,000, rotation can plausibly carry the range with a wind. Past ≈10,000, a 24-detent lap is 0.24% of the log and no rotation covers it — jumps stop being optional and the wheel becomes a precision tool by design.
  3. Does it have to stay a dial? The complaint is about precision, not about circles. A linear strip with momentum — the poster strip promoted to the control, the dial demoted — solves the same problem with maths the browser already has, at the cost of the one affordance a dial gives that a strip does not: an unbounded, non-scrolling control you can turn without the page moving.

11Assumptions

  • ≈800 events is taken from the report, not measured — nothing in the code carries a count.
  • The measurements of the current dial are from src/lib/components/history/time-dial.svelte as it stands on paul/mrq-276-time-travel: SWEEP = 180, RADIUS = 84, CENTRE = 120 in a 240 viewBox, ARC_TICKS = 9, a 320 ms settle, and the wheel handler's 40 px-per-detent accumulator. DETENT_CAPACITY = 120, densityFor, backStep and backFromProgress are from src/lib/sync/history.ts; the haptic timings (10 ms pulse, 40 ms floor) are from src/lib/client/history/haptics.svelte.ts; the strip's 20-title cap, −220 px translate and 520 ms settle are from src/routes/history/+page.svelte.
  • The page is client-only (IndexedDB, no server load), so no option here has an SSR constraint — nothing above runs on the server.
  • The dial is assumed to stay dumb about meaning: it emits deltas, the page composes labels. Options C and D break that assumption (the wheel would need to know what a lap or a sliver is), which is part of their cost.
  • Every number labelled proposal — 15° detents, 24 a turn, the 1.6 exponent, 400 events a second, the 280 ms decay, the haptic patterns — is chosen for coherence, not measured. The measured numbers are the ones with arithmetic against the current code.
